Rules & safety: fair pay, real neighbours, no scams.

Service Request is a bounty board for neighbourly jobs. Here's how paying works, how quality is judged, and how the app protects both sides.

Paying

  • You pay the helper directly: cash in hand, or an Interac e-Transfer from your bank. No money goes through the app and there are no fees.
  • The amount is set before the job: the bounty (at least $20), plus the quality bonus if the checklist score reaches the bar.
  • You pay after you validate the work, within 3 days. Until then, you can't post or hire again.
  • The helper's e-Transfer email is shown to you only after you validate their work, and only on that bounty.
  • Both sides confirm: you mark it paid (with the bank reference for e-Transfers), the helper confirms they got it.

Quality & bonus

Every bounty has a checklist of 2 to 8 things anyone can check: "bags at the curb", "plow ridge cleared". When the job is done, the helper sends photos and ticks what they did. The poster then ticks what they see.

The score is the share of items ticked. 4 of 5 is 80%. If the poster set a bonus, say $10 at 80%, the helper gets it from 80% up.

  • Unticking an item needs a written reason the helper can read.
  • If the poster doesn't check within 48 hours, the helper's own checklist counts.
  • Every poster's profile shows how often they pay the bonus, so tough graders are easy to spot.

Six scams to know

1
The overpayment trick

Someone "accidentally" sends more than the bounty and asks you to send the difference back. The first payment bounces or gets reversed; yours doesn't. Never send money back.

2
Fake e-Transfer emails

A real Interac e-Transfer shows up in your bank app. Don't click "deposit" links in emails or texts you weren't expecting, and never type your bank password on a link.

3
Pay to get the job

No real poster asks for a deposit, a fee, a uniform or a "registration". The app never asks you for money either.

4
Gift cards, crypto, cheques

Buying gift cards and sending the codes, crypto, or cashing a cheque for someone are classic scams. They're not allowed and the scanner flags them.

5
Carrying packages

Picking up or delivering sealed packages for a stranger can make you part of a fraud or worse. Not allowed.

6
Moving off the app too early

Scammers want your number before you're matched. Contact details are hidden until the poster picks you, so reports and the paper trail keep working.

What's allowed

Neighbourly jobs that need no degree and no licence.

Never allowed

A scanner reads every new bounty. Anything that looks risky waits for a moderator before it reaches the board.

Staying safe

  • Meet outside, in daylight, when you can. Tell someone where you're going.
  • You never have to go inside a home. If it feels wrong, leave and report it.
  • Under 18? Get a parent's OK first and let them see the bounty. (The app is for 16 and up.)
  • Use the poster's tools when the bounty says so, and take photos before and after.

Privacy

  • The board shows an area name and a circle about 500 m wide, never your address.
  • Your street address goes only to the helper you pick, once you pick them.
  • Profiles show a first name and an initial. Emails are never shown to anyone.
  • Phone numbers, emails and links in bounties and offers are hidden automatically.

Disputes & strikes

  • Can't sort it out in the chat? Either side can ask a moderator. The job is frozen until they decide what is owed.
  • An upheld report or a lost dispute is a strike. Three strikes pause the account.
  • New accounts can have 2 live bounties and 2 jobs on the go until they finish one.
  • Three reports from different neighbours take a bounty off the board until it's checked.
  • Reviews are blind until both sides write theirs, and each neighbour counts once in a rating.
